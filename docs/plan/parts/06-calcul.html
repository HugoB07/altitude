Calculation engine & performance

Data

This is where the product's credibility is won or lost. Users forgive an imperfect interface; they do not forgive a net worth that is €3,000 wrong.

6.1 The valuation chain

Computation runs in five passes, in this order, over a given date range:

#PassDetail
1Cash balancesRunning sum of entries.amount per account per day. A single SQL window function: sum(amount) OVER (PARTITION BY account_id ORDER BY booked_on).
2HoldingsSame principle over quantity, per (account, instrument) pair, after applying corporate actions (splits, consolidations).
3Price of the dayLast known close at that date (last observation carried forward), with a cap: beyond 7 calendar days without a quote, the value is flagged is_estimated.
4ConversionApplies the ECB rate for the day (or the last business day). The rate used is recorded, not recomputed: history must stay stable.
5AggregationRolls up the ltree path, weighted by ownership shares when consolidating by owner.
The weekend and public-holiday trap

Markets close; net worth does not. Showing only trading days produces a staircase curve and wrong calendar-period returns. Rule: the series is daily across all calendar days, with the last price carried forward; carried-forward points are flagged and rendered paler in charts.

6.2 Lots, cost basis and disposals

Every purchase creates a lot (quantity, unit price, fees, date, currency). Every sale consumes lots according to a per-account method:

MethodWhen to use itNote
Weighted average
PMP / CUMP		Default for France: it is the rule for brokerage accounts and the PEA.One cost basis per instrument per account. Simple, and it matches what a French user expects.
FIFOCrypto across jurisdictions, foreign accounts.Requires keeping lots individually. The lot_closures table records which lot settled which sale.
Specific identificationManual tax optimisation.The user picks lots at sale time. Rarely needed, but cheap to offer once lots exist.

Acquisition fees enter the cost basis; disposal fees reduce sale proceeds. Both remain separately visible for the total-cost-of-ownership report.

French wrappers to model from the start

PEA: there is no line-by-line taxable gain, only an overall gain on withdrawal. The account must know its opening date (the five-year rule) and cumulative contributions (the cap). Assurance-vie: a partial withdrawal is split pro rata between capital and gains — impossible to compute without a history of premiums. PER: deductible contributions must be tracked for the payout phase. These three fields live in accounts.attributes and are validated by a Zod schema keyed on subtype.

6.3 Performance measures

Three different numbers answering three different questions. Altitude shows them side by side, each named — never an anonymous "+12.4%".

MeasureAnswersMethod used
TWR
time-weighted		"Are my investment choices good?" — neutralises contributions.Geometric chaining of daily returns: Π (1 + (Vₜ − Vₜ₋₁ − Fₜ) / (Vₜ₋₁ + Fₜ)) − 1. This is the measure comparable to an index.
MWR / IRR
money-weighted		"What did my money actually earn me?" — accounts for contribution timing.XIRR: root-finding by Newton-Raphson, falling back to bisection on divergence. Degenerate cases (all flows same sign) are handled and flagged explicitly.
Modified DietzA robust approximation over a short period without daily valuation.Used for illiquid assets valued occasionally (real estate, private equity) where a daily TWR is meaningless.
Decision — test the engine against an external oracle

Portfolio Performance and the XIRR() function in Excel/LibreOffice act as oracles. Build roughly twenty reference scenarios (simple purchase, regular contributions, partial sale, dividend with withholding tax, split, foreign currency, a contribution on a sharp drawdown day) whose expected results are frozen in test fixtures. Tolerance: 10⁻⁶. These tests are the safety net that lets you refactor the engine without fear.

6.4 Geographic exposure — the data behind the Globe

The globe is not decorative: it reveals that a "well-diversified" portfolio is in fact 68% exposed to the United States. The figure comes from look-through:

-- Country weights across a portfolio subtree, at a given date
WITH pos AS (
  SELECT p.instrument_id,
         sum(p.quantity * pr.close * fx.rate) AS value_base
    FROM positions p
    JOIN accounts   a  ON a.id = p.account_id
    JOIN portfolios pf ON pf.id = a.portfolio_id
    JOIN instrument_prices pr ON …
    JOIN fx_rates fx ON …
   WHERE pf.path <@ 'home.investments'::ltree   -- ← the whole subtree
   GROUP BY 1
)
SELECT e.key AS country,
       sum(pos.value_base * e.weight) AS exposure,
       sum(pos.value_base * e.weight) / sum(sum(pos.value_base * e.weight)) OVER () AS pct
  FROM pos
  JOIN instrument_exposures e
    ON e.instrument_id = pos.instrument_id AND e.dimension = 'country'
 GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 2 DESC;

Fallback rules when exposure is unknown

  • Individual equity → issuer's country of incorporation, 100%.
  • ETF / fund with no data → country of domicile, marked not looked through, with a banner inviting the user to fill in the breakdown (a simple five-row form).
  • Real estate → the property's country, geocoded once at creation.
  • Cash → the country of the institution holding the account.
  • Crypto → a "no jurisdiction" bucket, shown off-globe in its own card. That is more honest than assigning it arbitrarily.

The share that could not be looked through is always displayed as such: a globe claiming to cover 100% while it only knows 60% is a visual lie.

Invalidation: bug source number one

Adding a transaction dated 2019 must invalidate daily_balances from 2019 to today for the accounts touched — and for those only. The implementation: a trigger feeds a recompute_queue(account_id, from_date) table, and the worker consumes it merging overlapping intervals. Never recompute inline inside an HTTP request: on a bulk import that is a minute of waiting and a held lock.
